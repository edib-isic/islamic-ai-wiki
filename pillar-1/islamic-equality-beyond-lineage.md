Let's talk about one of the most radical things Islam introduced to the world: real equality. Not the kind you say at rallies or put on posters. The kind you actually enforce when it costs you your power, your comfort, and your own tribe's loyalty.

When Muhammad conquered Mecca, after years of his own tribe trying to kill him, torturing his followers, blocking his message, he had absolute power over the Quraysh. They were used to being elite by blood. They thought they were above everyone else. They controlled trade, politics, and religion. He gathered them, looked at them, and said:

"Kurejševići! Allah je dokinuo predislamsku arogantnost i stepenovanje ljudi po porijeklu. Svi su ljudi potekli od Adema, a Adem od zemlje."

Qurayshites! Allah has abolished pre-Islamic arrogance and people ranking each other by ancestry. All humans descend from Adam, and Adam from dust.

That wasn't poetry. That wasn't a metaphor. That was a social revolution, delivered in one sentence, aimed directly at the power structure of seventh-century Arabia.

The Qur'an states it even more clearly:

"O ljudi, mi vas od jednog čovjeka i jedne žene stvaramo i na narode i plemena vas dijelimo da biste se upoznali. Najugledniji kod Allaha je onaj koji Ga se najviše boji. Allah, uistinu sve zna i ništa Mu nije skriveno." (49:13)

O mankind, We created you from a single pair, and made you into nations and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honorable of you in the sight of Allah is the most God-conscious.

Notice what's happening here. Islam doesn't say races and tribes shouldn't exist. It says they exist so you can know each other. Diversity is intentional. But it doesn't give anyone superiority. The measure is God-consciousness, nothing else.

And Islam didn't just say this. It built systems around it. It broke the old world by forcing these ideas into real life, in real situations, with real consequences.

## The Prophet's Own Family Didn't Get Special Treatment

Here's where the rubber meets the road. Anyone can talk about equality in theory. The test is whether you apply it to the people closest to you.

Muhammad addressed his uncle Abbas, his aunt Safiyya, and his daughter Fatimah — his most beloved family members:

"Abbase, sine Abdul-Muttaliba, ništa ti neću moći pomoći kod Allaha dž. š.; Safijja, strino Božijeg poslanika, ništa ti neću moći pomoći kod Allaha dž. š.; Fatima, kćeri Muhammedova, traži šta hoćeš od moje imovine, ali ti ništa neću pomoći kod Allaha."

Abbas, son of Abdul-Muttalib, I cannot help you with Allah. Safiyya, aunt of the Messenger, I cannot help you with Allah. Fatimah, daughter of Muhammad, ask me for whatever you want from my wealth, but I cannot help you with Allah.

He's clear. Family status means nothing when it comes to your relationship with God. You can't inherit salvation. You can't use your connection to get special treatment. His uncle. His aunt. His daughter, the person he loved more than anyone else in the world. No exceptions.

Then he said something even more striking about Fatimah:

"Tako mi Allaha, kada bi ukrala Fatima, kći Muhammedova, odsjekao bih joj ruku."

By Allah, if Fatimah, the daughter of Muhammad, were to steal, I would cut off her hand.

This wasn't a hypothetical statement made in private. It was delivered publicly, recorded, and became Islamic law. No noble goes unpunished. No commoner goes unprotected. The Quraysh had a system where thieves escaped if they were elite and the poor were punished if they weren't. The Prophet called that out explicitly and said that's exactly how nations collapse:

"Narodi, koji su bili prije vas, stradali su, jer su kradljivca — plemića, puštali nekažnjena, a kada bi ukrao običan građanin, kažnjavali bi ga."

Nations before you perished because they let the noble thief go unpunished, but when an ordinary citizen stole, they punished him.

That's the root of corruption. That's the root of social decay. And Islam identified it, named it, and rejected it.

## "Family" Is Redefined

The Quraysh measured family by blood. Islam measured it by character.

Bilal ibn Rabah was a black slave. When he embraced Islam, the Quraysh tortured him — laying him on burning sand, placing a heavy stone on his chest, demanding he abandon his faith. After he was freed, the Prophet didn't treat him as "former slave." He made Bilal the first muezzin, the voice that calls Muslims to prayer every day. The most trusted, honored role in the community.

And when the dialogue in this book asks whether Bilal and others like him could really be considered part of the Prophet's family, the answer is direct:

"Islam povlači znak jednakosti između svih ljudi i inzistira na poštivanju jednakosti. Muslimani su kao češljevi zubci..."

Islam draws a line of equality among all people and insists on respecting equality. Muslims are like the teeth of a comb...

Equal. The same height. The same value. No one tooth is superior.

Selman al-Farisi was a Persian foreigner — a nobody in Arab tribal politics. The Prophet said: "Selman je od nas, Ahl al-Bajt." Selman is of us, the Ahl al-Bayt, the family of the Prophet.

Abu Hurairah was extremely poor. The Prophet treated him as close as any noble companion. His enemies complained: "Kako da ti vjerujemo, kad te bijedni i prezreni slijede?" How can we believe you when the wretched and despised follow you?

But Allah called the Prophet's companions the most moral and most sincere. The Prophet said: "Moji su drugovi kao i zvijezde; kojeg god uzmete za uzor i putokaz, nećete zalutati." My companions are like the stars — whichever one you follow as a guide, you will not go astray.

And he added: "Svaki bogobojazni član je Resulullahove porodice." Every God-fearing person is a member of the Messenger of Allah's family.

So who is family in Islam? Not the person with the right last name. Not the person with the right bloodline. The person with the right character.

## Tribalism Called Out Directly

Someone asked the Prophet what asabiyyah, tribal loyalty, means. He answered:

"Da pomažeš i podržavaš svoga kada i nepravdu čini."

Helping your group commit injustice.

In one sentence, Islam dismantled the excuse that "loyalty to your people" justifies covering up their wrongs. That concept — protecting your own no matter what — is at the root of corruption, nepotism, and injustice today. Islam rejected it outright.

Islam commands you to judge based on justice, not based on who someone is related to. The Prophet's own tribe, the Quraysh, had built their entire power structure on this tribal loyalty. You protect your family. You protect your clan. You don't betray your own.

Islam said: that's not loyalty. That's injustice.

And it's even clearer in the Qur'an:

"Samo su vjernici braća."

Only the believers are brothers.

You're not brothers because you share blood. You're brothers because you share faith and commitment to justice.

## Even Close Relatives Got No Mercy

The Prophet's own uncle, Abu Lahab, and his wife Ummi Jamil were among his cruelest enemies. Abu Lahab threw thorns in the Prophet's path. Ummi Jamil scattered dust on his head and mocked him openly. They used their family connection as a shield to do whatever they wanted.

Despite being blood family, the Qur'an declared:

"Neka propadne Ebu Leheb, i propao je! Neće mu biti od koristi blago njegovo, a ni ono što je stekao." (111:1-2)

May the hands of Abu Lahab perish, and he himself is ruined. Neither his wealth nor what he gained will benefit him.

The Prophet's own uncle. Cursed in the Qur'an. That's how Islam treats betrayal of truth — no family protection, no tribal immunity, no blood excuse.

The book notes: "Njih je spojilo krvno srodstvo, ali ih je razdvojilo stričevo nakaradno ponašanje. Zato će biti odgovoran i kažnjen, makar se radilo o Hašimoviću, Abdul-Mutalliboviću, plemenu kojem pripada i sam Muhammed a.s."

Blood connected them. But the uncle's monstrous behavior separated them. He will be held accountable and punished, even though he was a Hashimite, an Abdul-Muttalibite, from the same tribe as Muhammad himself.

## Slavery — Not Defended, But Dismantled

When Islam arrived, slavery existed everywhere on Earth. It was the economic backbone of every civilization. Islam didn't pretend it didn't exist. It didn't defend it. It made its abolition a religious duty.

The book explains: "Kada je islam objavljen, našao je društveni poredak koji se zasnivao na ropstvu kao okosnici socijalnog uređenja, i to ne samo u krajevima gdje se islam pojavio, nego u težem ili lakšem obliku, u cijelom svijetu."

When Islam was revealed, it found a social order based on slavery as the cornerstone of social organization, not only in the regions where Islam appeared, but in heavier or lighter forms, throughout the entire world.

Islam didn't ignore this. It worked within it to dismantle it from the inside.

The Prophet said:

"Ko oslobodi roba vjernika, biće mu to iskupljenje od vatre."

Whoever frees a believing slave, that is their ransom from the Fire.

He commanded masters to feed slaves as they feed themselves, clothe them as they clothe themselves:

"Hranite ih kao što sebe hranite, odijevajte ih kao što sebe odijevate."

Feed them as you feed yourselves, clothe them as you clothe yourselves.

And if a master beat a slave unjustly:

"Ko bi nepravedno izudarao, ili ošamario svoga roba, njegova vjerska otkupna obaveza je da ga oslobodi."

Whoever unjustly strikes or slaps his slave, his religious expiation is to free him.

Over time, the religious, economic, and social incentives Islam created made slavery collapse in Muslim societies long before Europe did.

This wasn't just charity. It was justice.

## The Four Pillars of the Message

The Prophet's entire mission can be reduced to four foundations:

1. Belief in One God — not tribal idols, not national gods, one God for all people
2. Equality — all humans equal in origin and dignity
3. Justice — no exception for your family, your tribe, your class
4. Freedom — liberation from slavery, from ignorance, from oppression

The book states this directly: "To se može sažeti u četiri osnovne tačke: 1) vjerovanje u Jednog Jedinstvenog; 2) jednakost među svim ljudima; 3) pravednost, i 4) sloboda."

These weren't abstract ideas. They were the opposite of everything the old order stood for. Every single one of them threatened the power structure of seventh-century Arabia.

## Justice Is the Highest Standard

Islam demands justice even when it's inconvenient. Even when it hurts your side.

The Qur'an says:

"O vjernici, budite pravedni, svjedoci Allahu, makar protiv vas samih, ili protiv roditelja i rođaka." (4:135)

O believers, be steadfast in justice, witnesses for Allah, even if it is against yourselves, your parents, or your relatives.

Even against your own family. That's how far justice goes.

And Islam treats the weak first. Not the strong. Orphans, the poor, widows, travelers — they're not afterthoughts. They're central. The Prophet said:

"I ja i skrbnik siročeta u Džennetu ćemo biti ovako," stavljajući prste zajedno.

I and the caretaker of an orphan will be in Paradise like this, holding his fingers together.

Caretaker of an orphan. That's the image of closeness to God.

## What This Looks Like Today

Racism is modern asabiyyah. Same mechanism, different label. You're protecting your group and judging others by something irrelevant to their worth.

When laws bend for the rich but punish the poor, that's exactly the corruption the Prophet warned would destroy civilizations.

When someone's dignity is reduced because of their race, nationality, or social status — that's the pre-Islamic arrogance Islam abolished.

When privilege goes unchallenged, when nepotism is defended as "taking care of our own," that's tribal loyalty protecting injustice.

When a community ignores the vulnerable among them while claiming faith — that's what the Qur'an condemns when it says:

"Woe to those who pray but are heedless, who seek praise and withhold kindness." (107:4-6)

## The Standard

Islam's equality isn't a slogan. It's a system. And it started by cutting off the hands of your own daughter if she stole, freeing your slaves, and telling your own tribe that their bloodline no longer gives them superiority.

That's the standard. Measure everything against it.
